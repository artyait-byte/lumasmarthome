Types of Security Cameras We Install in Sarasota

Every camera we fit comes from Ubiquiti's UniFi Protect line, so one recorder and one app run them all. The right type depends on what a position has to see, how far away, and how exposed it is.

A long camera that points one way and sees far. The UniFi G6 Bullet, or the G6 Pro Bullet with optical zoom, is our usual pick for a driveway, a side yard or a long property line, with infrared for full darkness.

Best for: driveways, side gates, docks and long yards.

A low-profile dome that sits flush on a ceiling or under a soffit. The lens hides behind the cover, so it is hard to tell where it points, and it reads as part of the house rather than a gadget.

Best for: front entries, covered lanais, garages and porches.

A ball-shaped camera on a short mount. The UniFi G6 Turret has no glass bubble in front of the lens, so its own infrared does not glare at night and there is no cover to fog in the humidity.

Best for: under eaves, back doors and pool areas.

The UniFi G6 180 sees a full 180° from one point, without the blind seam two cameras leave between them. One camera on a corner can watch a whole back yard or the width of a motor court.

Best for: wide yards, motor courts and pool decks.

A dome in a metal housing with an IK10 impact rating, for any camera someone could reach. It keeps recording after a kick or a thrown object, which matters on a ground-floor garage or a gate post.

Best for: garages, gate posts and anything within reach.

UniFi's AI LPR camera reads plates where cars slow down, day and night. For a private house it is only worth it on a long gated drive or a shared private road.

Best for: gated drives and private roads.

A UniFi Protect video doorbell shows the face at the door, records deliveries and lets you answer from your phone. It is wired, so there is no battery to fail in August, and it records with the other cameras.

Best for: front doors and side entries.

Choosing the Right Camera for Your Property

Resolution comes first. A 4K camera lets you zoom into a recording and still identify a face or a plate; 2K is enough for a porch or a hallway where people pass close. More pixels also mean more storage, so we mix the two by position.

Field of view trades against distance: a wide lens covers the whole yard but makes a face at the far end small. Night vision matters more than people expect. Infrared on UniFi outdoor cameras reaches roughly 100 ft or more, and landscape lighting brings back colour at night.

Retention sets the recorder. Storage is cameras × bitrate × hours × days, so a four-camera house fits on one drive while a twelve-camera site needs a multi-drive recorder. Tell us how many days you want to keep and we size it.

Wired PoE beats Wi-Fi and battery cameras in Florida: no batteries failing in the heat, no Wi-Fi dropouts across a lanai, one cable for power and video. A Wi-Fi camera is the fallback for a spot no cable can reach.

All UniFi hardware we fit is NDAA-compliant. Audio is off by default: Florida requires the consent of everyone in a conversation before it is recorded (Fla. Stat. 934.03), so camera microphones stay switched off.

Security cameras in Sarasota: common questions

How much does security camera installation cost in Sarasota?

Our packages start from $2,500 for 4 cameras (Home 4), from $4,200 for 8 cameras (Office 8) and from $10,900 for 12 or more (Business Pro), plus tax. Each includes the cameras, a UniFi recorder and the installation. The final fixed price follows a free walk of the property.

How many security cameras does a house need?

Most Sarasota houses need four to eight: the driveway, the front door, the back yard or lanai, and each side gate. Waterfront homes often add one for the dock. We count them with you on the walk.

Is there a monthly fee?

No. Video records to a UniFi recorder on your property, with no subscription and no per-camera fee. Alarm monitoring is a separate option, only if you ask for it.

How long does installation take?

Most homes and small businesses are installed in one day. Larger sites, long cable runs and gates take longer; the camera plan states the number of days before you commit.

Wired or wireless cameras for a Florida home?

Wired PoE wherever a cable can reach. Batteries struggle in the heat and Wi-Fi drops across lanais and block walls. A wireless camera is the fallback for one spot no cable can reach.

Can you replace or upgrade my existing cameras?

Usually, yes. If the old cameras run on network cable (Cat5e or Cat6), we can often reuse it for new PoE cameras, which saves time and drywall. Older coax systems usually need new cable. We check on the walk and tell you what can stay.

Do your cameras record audio?

Audio is off by default. Florida law requires the consent of everyone in a conversation before it is recorded (Fla. Stat. 934.03), so we leave camera microphones switched off.

Are UniFi cameras NDAA-compliant?

Yes. The UniFi Protect cameras and recorders we install are NDAA-compliant.

Do you install cameras for businesses and HOAs?

Yes: offices, shops, restaurants, warehouses and associations. See business security camera systems in Sarasota and HOA and condo security cameras.
